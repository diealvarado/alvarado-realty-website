## What the 20% circuit breaker actually limits

The cap limits the *appraised value* used to calculate property taxes. It does not cap your tax rate or freeze the bill. Local taxing units still set rates, and appraisal districts still determine market value as of January 1.

Under Tax Code §23.231, the appraisal office may not raise the appraised value above the lesser of the property's market value, or last year's appraised value plus 20 percent of that value plus the market value of all new improvements. A "new improvement" is an improvement made after the most recent appraisal that increases market value and was not already in last year's appraised value — not ordinary repairs or maintenance. Replacement structures after a casualty or wind or water damage have special rules. Your notice of appraised value must say whether the property qualifies.

## Who qualifies — and when it starts

The limitation applies only to real property that is not a qualifying residence homestead, is not receiving special appraisal (including agricultural land, timberland, recreational, park and scenic land, public access airport property, or restricted-use timberland), and has an appraised value of not more than a Comptroller-set threshold in the tax year the property first qualifies. The Comptroller has published those amounts as **$5,000,000** for 2024, **$5,160,000** for 2025, and **$5,320,000** for 2026. Check the value and qualification statement on your notice, including in [Coppell](/areas/coppell/), [Trophy Club](/areas/trophy-club/), [Highland Village](/areas/highland-village/), [Bedford](/areas/bedford/), and [Euless](/areas/euless/). Acreage with agricultural special appraisal is not covered.

You do not apply. Tarrant Appraisal District states that it applies the limitation automatically. Dallas and Collin appraisal districts describe the same statutory cap. Denton County properties follow the same state law; confirm your notice with Denton CAD. The Comptroller notes that you may protest a denial.

The cap takes effect on January 1 of the tax year after the first tax year the owner owns the property on January 1 — not in that first January 1 year. Pre-2023 acquisitions are treated as January 1, 2023, so the cap could first apply on January 1, 2024. A sale resets it the following January 1; Tarrant Appraisal District says the tax base then increases to current market value until the new owner is eligible. If your first January 1 of ownership is in 2026, the cap would otherwise start on January 1, 2027, after the scheduled expiration. Ask your appraisal district and a CPA about your closing date.

## How this differs from the 10% homestead cap

A qualifying residence homestead has a separate 10% limitation under Tax Code §23.23. It starts the January 1 after homestead qualification, ends when that exemption no longer applies, has no value threshold, and is not scheduled to expire with the circuit breaker. The two caps do not stack. Converting to or from a rental can change which rule applies — ask your CPA and the appraisal district.

| | 10% homestead cap (§23.23) | 20% circuit breaker (§23.231) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual limit | 10% plus new improvements, never above market | 20% plus new improvements, never above market |
| Applies to | Qualifying residence homestead | Eligible non-homestead real property at or below the threshold |
| Value ceiling | None | $5,000,000 (2024), $5,160,000 (2025), $5,320,000 (2026) |

## What North DFW rental owners should weigh now

The law is statewide. Your notice and taxing units are local.

**1. Read the 2026 notice.** It should state market and appraised values and whether the property qualifies. A gap between market value and the capped appraised value is this year's holdback, not a predicted 2027 bill.

**2. The cap is scheduled to end this year.** Voters approved temporary constitutional authority on November 7, 2023 (Proposition 4, H.J.R. 2). Senate Bill 2 added §23.231, effective January 1, 2024, for tax years 2024 through 2026. The statute and the constitutional provisions expire December 31, 2026. As of November 24, 2026, the Comptroller still lists that expiration. Unless the Legislature extends the law, the cap is not scheduled to apply in 2027, and a capped property can be appraised at market value under whatever law is then in effect.

**3. Do not project a tax bill from this page.** Rates, exemptions, and 2027 market value are not known here. A CPA or property-tax consultant can model this from *your* 2026 notice.

## FAQs

**Does the 20% cap apply automatically to my rental?**
If the property qualifies, yes. Tarrant Appraisal District says no application is required. Check your notice; the Comptroller says you may protest a denial.

**What if I just bought the property, or I sell it?**
The cap starts the January 1 after the first tax year you own the property on January 1, and it ends when ownership changes. Ask your appraisal district and CPA how a 2025 or 2026 purchase is treated.

**Will my 2027 appraisal jump if the cap expires?**
Not by a number this page can invent. If the cap is not extended, 2027 appraisals can be at market value. Your bill depends on that value, exemptions, and rates — a question for your CPA.
